### r/indiehackers

**Title:** Shipped a Chrome extension in July, told nobody until late August. Here's what 6 weeks of pure store-search traffic looks like.

**Body:**

Numbers first, because that's why you're here.

**Bokal** — an open-source cookie manager for Chrome. Free tier is the whole tool; one paid feature at $29.99 one-time / $4.99 mo.

| | |
|---|---|
| Live on Chrome Web Store | 15 Jul 2026 |
| Users | 13 → 26 → 51 → **56** |
| Revenue | **$0** |
| GitHub stars | **0** |
| Marketing posts made | **0** |
| Marketing spend | **$0** |

Every one of those installs is organic Chrome Web Store search. Somebody typed "cookie editor", scrolled past the 2M-user incumbent, and picked mine.

**The interesting bit:** roughly doubling with zero distribution suggests the listing converts on impressions. That's the half most launches get wrong — they buy attention and the listing doesn't close. I have the opposite problem, which I'm told is the better one to have.

**The embarrassing bit:** I wrote a complete launch kit on 16 July — Show HN draft, subreddit-by-subreddit rules research, Product Hunt copy — and then posted **none of it** for five weeks. Writing the launch kit *felt* like launching. It isn't. I think I used polish as a way to avoid the uncomfortable part, which is walking into a room and saying "I made this, look at it."

**Three things I've learned that might be worth something to you:**

1. **Store search is a real channel and it's badly underrated.** No posts, no backlinks, no spend, and it still roughly doubled. If you're shipping into an app store, the listing copy is worth more of your attention than your launch tweet.
2. **Verify your differentiators against the competitor's actual source before you write copy.** I discovered — the morning I meant to post — that the incumbent is also GPL-3.0 and has used optional host permissions since 2023. Two of my three headline claims were table stakes. Twenty minutes of reading their manifest would have saved me from finding out in a comment thread.
3. **$0 at 56 installs is not a pricing signal.** At any realistic 1–4% conversion that predicts under one sale. I nearly redesigned the paywall over a number that contained no information. The threshold where it starts to mean something is more like 150–200 installs.
